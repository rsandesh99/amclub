**Monetisation observed:** Udaan's public pages disclose no commission/take-rate percentage. Revenue signals are indirect: wholesale-margin trade (FMCG/Pharma) plus a captive lending arm, udaanCapital, monetising working-capital credit to the same buyer base (interest rate and credit limit not disclosed on any public page reached).

**Top 3 to adopt for AMC Mart:**
1. **Category-tiered return-window disclosure** (e.g., fabrication consumables get a short damage-report window, safety/PPE items get a longer defect window) — problem: buyers don't know how long they have to flag a bad delivery, driving avoidable disputes. Metric moved: dispute rate <5%. Effort: S. Principle check: escrow kept (yes — return window sits inside the existing escrow release logic); no off-platform leakage (yes); no negotiation/auction (yes). Main risk: setting windows too short for fabrication goods (which may need on-site inspection) inflates disputes instead of reducing them.
2. **One-tap reorder for repeat consumables** (e.g., recurring welding rod / abrasive orders) — problem: MSME buyers re-purchase the same MRO SKUs monthly but re-navigate the catalog each time. Metric moved: 90-day repeat ≥25%. Effort: S/M. Principle check: escrow kept; no leakage; no negotiation — clean. Main risk: low, mostly a UI/data feature; risk is stale pricing shown at reorder time if not GST-refreshed.
3. **NBFC-partnered short-term purchase credit** (Udaan-Capital-style, but partner-funded, not own-book) — problem: Kurnool fabrication buyers often need 15–30 day float to bridge production-to-payment cycles. Metric moved: 90-day repeat ≥25%, and indirectly take rate 10–12% via larger basket sizes. Effort: L. Principle check: escrow kept only if credit disbursal still routes through platform escrow to seller (must design carefully); no off-platform leakage — risk if NBFC settles directly with seller outside platform; no negotiation/auction — clean. Main risk: this is explicitly labeled LATER in the brief (NBFC credit) — treat as a roadmap item, not a near-term build, and the credit-risk/compliance overhead is real.

**Anti-patterns:**
- Udaan's return policy places return-shipping and dispute execution risk on the buyer/seller pair directly ("your sole responsibility to return the Product directly to the Seller," "no guarantee ... for such return") — weakens the platform's mediating role that AMClub's escrow model is built to provide (Observed-public: udaan.com/pages/udaan-return-faq/md).
- No visible SLA for delivery time undermines buyer trust at checkout — a vague promise ("Fast & Reliable Delivery") without a stated day count is a weaker pattern than a committed SLA (Observed-public: udaan.com).

**Monetisation observed:** The "save up to 18%" GST/ITC framing is repeated verbatim across at least two distinct AZB pages (the GST feature page and the savings/discounts page), indicating it is Amazon Business India's primary acquisition hook for GST-registered buyers. Additional stacked monetisation/retention levers seen: Subscribe & Save (up to 10% extra), a Premium loyalty points program, monthly "Business Value Days," and Amazon Pay Later financing for bulk orders. No commission/take-rate percentage charged to sellers was found on any public buyer-facing page (expected — that data lives on seller-central pages, out of scope for this survey).

**Top 3 to adopt for AMC Mart:**
1. **Explicit "GST invoice ≠ automatic ITC eligibility" disclosure** on any listing where this applies — problem: buyers may assume any GST invoice guarantees ITC and get a compliance surprise. Metric moved: dispute rate <5%, review ≥4.2. Effort: S (a display/copy change on top of the already-planned ITC-aware display). Principle check: clean on all three (escrow, leakage, negotiation). Main risk: low; mainly a content/legal-review task to get the GST-slab logic right.
2. **24-hour GST invoice availability SLA** — problem: buyers need the invoice quickly to file ITC claims on their own GST cycle; an undefined wait erodes trust. Metric moved: review ≥4.2, dispute rate <5%. Effort: M (depends on seller-side invoice generation speed, which the brief already scopes as "invoice/e-way bill drafting," built/off). Principle check: clean. Main risk: sellers in the Kurnool cluster may be slower/less digitally equipped than Amazon's seller base, so a 24-hour SLA may need to start as a target, not a guarantee.
3. **Structured, capped "request additional discount" flow for un-tiered SKUs** — problem: buyers on odd-quantity or high-value fabrication orders want a better price than the listed tier, but AMClub's principles forbid open per-buyer negotiation. Design as a bounded, single-round RFQ-style discount request (reusing the existing goods-RFQ mechanics: unit price + GST slab + HSN, capped revisions) rather than AZB's open-ended "request from multiple suppliers." Metric moved: take rate 10–12% (larger baskets convert instead of abandoning). Effort: M. Principle check: no negotiation/auction — this is the one to watch closely; must stay a structured, capped RFQ, not free-form haggling, to remain compliant with AMClub's stated principles. Main risk: if implemented loosely, it collapses into exactly the "per-buyer negotiation" pattern the brief lists as NOT-NOW.

**Anti-patterns:**
- "Request a discount from multiple suppliers" for items lacking a published quantity discount is functionally a negotiation/haggling channel — directly conflicts with AMClub's stated NOT-NOW principle of "per-buyer negotiation" and should not be copied without the guardrails described above (Observed-public: business.amazon.in/en/key-features/reduce-costs/savings-discounts).
- Amazon Business Premium's loyalty reward points program matches the brief's explicit NOT-NOW "gamification points" — noted here only as an anti-pattern to avoid, per existing AMClub policy (Observed-public: business.amazon.in/en/key-features/reduce-costs/savings-discounts).
- Inconsistent catalog-size claims across AZB's own pages ("16 crore+" vs "15 crore+") is a small but real credibility anti-pattern worth avoiding in AMClub's own public copy (Observed-public: comparing the two fetched pages).

**Not verified:**
- India-specific "Pay by Invoice"/Amazon Pay Later exact terms (interest rate, eligibility, net-term length) — WebSearch results were dominated by US (amazon.com) Pay-by-Invoice/Net-30 content; no India-specific rate card was located or fetched.
